Insurance in six ideas

Insurer vs broker

The insurer (e.g. one of the 4 panel companies) promises to pay if something goes wrong and carries the risk. A broker like TISPH sells and arranges that cover for the customer and earns commission. TISPH never pays claims from its own money.

Policy & premium

A policy is the contract. The premium is the price. For Motor, the premium is based on the car's Fair Market Value and the perils covered (own damage, theft, acts of nature, third-party liability).

Quotation → policy

A quotation is a price offer, valid for 30 days. Once the customer accepts and pays, TISPH places it with the insurer, who issues the policy.

Endorsement

Any change during the policy term: new address, new owner, extra cover. Changes that affect price create an extra charge or refund, worked out pro-rata (by days left).

Renewal, lapse, cancellation

Policies usually last one year. Renewal starts a new one. Missed payment can suspend then lapse it. Cancellation ends it early, often with a pro-rata refund.

Claim & remittance

A claim is a request for payment after a loss (first report = FNOL). Remittance is TISPH passing collected premium to the insurer, minus its commission.

One customer, start to finish

An illustrative example built from the BRD's rules. Maria is not a real customer.

  1. Day 0
    Maria buys a Toyota Vios on a car loan. Toyota Financial Services sends her loan details to BrokerVerse as a lead. M03 Leads
  2. Day 1–3
    System assigns her to a sales agent tied to one insurer. The agent must call within 3 days, or the lead is reassigned. M03
  3. Day 3
    Agent raises a Motor quotation priced on the car's Fair Market Value, with own damage, theft, acts of nature and CTPL. Valid 30 days. M02 · M05
  4. Day 10
    Maria accepts and pays. Finance issues an acknowledgement receipt and matches the money to her debit note. M14 Collections
  5. Day 11
    Operations approves (a different person from the agent) and sends placement to the insurer. M06 Placement
  6. Day 13
    Insurer issues the e-policy. Operations checks it against the quote slip and books it. Maria's policy is now In force. M06
  7. Next week
    Finance remits her premium to the insurer in the weekly run, minus TISPH's commission. A daily file goes to SAP. M15 · M17
  8. Month 5
    Maria moves house. A non-financial endorsement updates her address after insurer confirmation. M10 Endorsements
  9. Month 8
    Minor accident. She reports it (FNOL), submits the document checklist, the insurer issues a Letter of Authority for repair, and the claim is settled. M13 Claims
  10. Month 9–12
    Renewal notices go out at 90, 60 and 30 days. Her renewal quote is re-rated on the car's lower value. M12 Renewals

02 · Business model

TISPH is a broker, not an insurer

It finds the business, arranges cover, collects premium and services the customer. It does not carry risk and does not issue policies. That one fact shapes every process.

Where customers come from

  • Toyota Financial Services: about 95% of leads, with loan details attached
  • Dealer network, referrals, walk-ins, direct enquiries
  • Credit Life: monthly TFS batch of ~8,000 loan accounts
→

TISPH (the broker)

  • Captures and qualifies leads
  • Quotes, places cover with insurer
  • Collects premium, remits to insurer
  • Earns commission
  • Services endorsements, renewals, claims
→

Insurer panel (4 insurers)

  • Carries the risk
  • Confirms issuance and issues the e-policy
  • Each agent is tied to one insurer, so the insurer is mostly decided before quoting
Rule 1: the insurer makes it liveA policy is live only when the insurer confirms issuance. No screen lets a TISPH user complete cover.
Rule 2: maker never approvesWhoever raises a quotation, endorsement, remittance or disbursement can never approve it.

12 · Before you sign

Points worth checking in the document

Small inconsistencies spotted while building this guide.

Phase 1 customization count differs in three places. Section 3 says 62, section 7 says 60, and the Phase 1 customization table actually lists 56. The gap is the 6 management dashboards: v2.2 moved them to Phase 2, but CR-17 still lists them as a Phase 1 change request.
Five finance reports have two phases. FGA-04, 08, 09, 10 and 11 are Phase 1 (build) in the report list (6.4) but Phase 2 in the customization list (7.2). Marked “Phase 2*” in the reference lists.
Non-functional gaps. NFR-05 is used for two different requirements, and NFR-07.5 (batch processing time) is still “TBD c/o iorta”.
Tax items still described as out of scope. TAX-02 and TAX-03 are confirmed for Phase 1, but their customization rows still say “Out of scope per the BRD — indicative only”.
Phase figure is out of date. Figure 20 shows 22 Phase 1 build items and 2 out of scope. The requirement tables show 24 Phase 1 build items and none out of scope, because TAX-02 and TAX-03 were moved into Phase 1. The bars in section 07 follow the requirement tables.
A requirement is missing. Module 07 says it has 13 requirements, but PJRN-12 (Marine, per Figure 20) has no row in the BRD. Only 149 rows are actually written out.
Version label. The change history shows v2.2 (29 Sep 2026), but the cover and page headers still read v2.0 (18 Sep 2026).
Open items owed by TISPH. Several requirements still note pending inputs, e.g. scheme pricing parameters (CR-05), the sharing-scheme schedule, the budget master for the variance report, and insurer rates for Pioneer and Maagap.
Leftover editing note. Section 4.1 contains the text “update the parcel/courier to be part of the required product”, which reads like a reviewer comment left in the final.
